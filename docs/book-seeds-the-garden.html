The garden

Garden paths

Lay brick, gravel or crazy paving in place of grass; stepping stones and log rounds sit on the lawn.

Herringbone Brick PathGravel PathCrazy PavingStepping StoneLog Round

Left alone, paths green over: moss in shade, creeping thyme in sun (it flowers in summer). Feet wear it back.

A hoe scrapes it out; bone meal brings it on.

Picket fences

Picket fences join gates and other wooden fences. One dye repaints a whole run; an axe strips it bare.

→4

Woven hazel hurdles: six sticks make two.

Pots and planters

A terracotta pot or long planter takes any small plant: a flower, a herb, a fern, a sapling.

Terracotta PotLong Planter

Sown crops grow on; pick one ripe with an empty hand and it starts again.

Crouch with an empty hand to lift a plant out.

Sitting out

Garden benches side by side join into one long bench. On a fine day with flowers about, a sit in the sun mends you, and the butterflies come.

Garden BenchString of Jar Lanterns

Click any spot on a post, fence or wall with jar lanterns, then another: the string hangs between, lit at dusk.

Seed trays

Set a paper pot in each cell of a seed tray, fill it with dirt, sow one seed, and keep it watered.

Paper PotSeed TrayPotting BenchPotted Seedling

On a potting bench, twice as fast, winter or not. Planted out, a seedling starts well grown.

Left dry too long, seedlings wilt and die.

The water butt

A water butt catches the rain. Buckets, bottles and the watering can fill from it.

Water ButtWatering Can

Rainwater is what seedlings like best.

The glasshouse

Under glass, crops grow a third faster, and winter can't touch them.

Glasshouse PanelGlasshouse RoofGlasshouse RidgeGlasshouse Door

Stack panels for the walls, roof pieces facing in for the slopes, a ridge on top.

Fill the gable ends with panels; they join the roof.

Heating

A paraffin heater burns coal or charcoal and keeps a glasshouse warm all winter. Light it with flint and steel.

Paraffin HeaterGlasshouse Thermometer

Hang a thermometer on the wall to see how warm it is.

Tender crops

Cucumbers, sweet peppers, aubergines and grapes want 16 degrees: a glasshouse, or high summer.

CucumberSweet PepperAubergineGrapes

Below 6 degrees the frost knocks them back.

The hedgehog pedlar sells their seeds.

The grapevine

Plant a cutting and it climbs its post. Pick the bunches by hand and it fruits again.

Grapevine CuttingRaisinsGrape JuiceRed Wine

Dry grapes on the rack for raisins; press them for juice, and leave it to become wine.

From the glasshouse

Ratatouille simmers in the pot. Stuffed peppers and moussaka are mixed in the bowl and baked.

Greek SaladRatatouilleStuffed Peppers
MoussakaJar of Pickled GherkinsJar of Chutney

Gherkins pickle in honey vinegar; chutney keeps all winter.

Garden buildings

Weatherboard builds sheds and summerhouses. Dye repaints a wall or a roof.

WeatherboardWeatherboard StairsWishing WellDovecote

Draw water from a wishing well, or drop in a gold nugget and make a wish.

Doves move into a dovecote.
