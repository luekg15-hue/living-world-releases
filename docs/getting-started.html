Getting started

Wildhearth turns Minecraft into a living countryside: seasons that change the land, soil that tires, animals with real genes, orchards that need bees, a proper kitchen. Here's how to find your feet in your first few days.

Read the Field Guide

Every new player is handed the Naturalist's Field Guide on joining. Right-click to read it: it explains everything as you meet it, and this wiki is built from it word for word. The other books (cattle, pigs, poultry, sheep, bees, seeds, caves and the kitchen) are crafted from a book and something fitting; their recipes are on the recipes page. A wall bookshelf keeps them handy.

Your first days

  1. Mind the season

    Worlds start in spring. Each season lasts 14 days unless your server changes it, and winter is cold: snow, frost, and most crops waiting for spring. See the year round.

  2. Dig a garden

    Hoe some ground and look at it with the hoe in hand to read its fertility. Fresh farmland is 6 out of 10. Every harvest takes some out, so rotate your crops and add compost.

  3. Find some livestock

    Wild cows, pigs, chickens and sheep come in real breeds. Hold their food and look at one to read its breed and genes. You need a male and a female to breed them. Browse them all in the breed database.

  4. Build a trough

    A feeding trough keeps animals fed and fattens them: a Prime animal gives up to three extra meat.

  5. Plant an orchard

    Fruit tree leaves drop saplings now and then. Blossom only sets fruit if it's pollinated, so keep bees near your trees. Warm-country trees (lemon, mandarin, olive, avocado) keep growing after you plant them.

  6. Cook properly

    The Cook's Companion shows the knife and board, mortar, mixing bowl, frying pan and cooking pot. Eating something new fills you more than the same dish again.

Tip Hold a tool or food and look at things: a hoe reads soil, feed reads animals, apples or berries read deer.

Out in the wild

The woods hold deer whose genes you can breed for, woodland folk (reindeer, porcupines, badgers, chipmunks and forest elves), wild boar, crows that raid your crops, and eight kinds of wild berries. Rivers and lakes have their own fish: see fishing. Underground there's a whole other world: read the Caver's Handbook before you go.

Mark your calendar

  • Midsummer Eve (the middle day of summer): build a bonfire; it lights itself at dusk and the village dances. Leap the flames for a year's luck.
  • All Hallows Eve (four nights before the fair): follow a will-o'-the-wisp in the dark to a gift.
  • The Harvest Fair (the last day of autumn): Judge Bramblewick visits every show bench and the prizes are given at noon.

The almanac shows what's in flower and fruit at every point of the year.
