The year round

A Wildhearth year has four seasons, and the land changes with each: the colour of the grass and leaves, the weather, what grows and what ripens, and the festivals.

How long is a year?

Each season lasts 14 days by default (a server can set anything from 1 to 120 with days_per_season in config/livingworld.properties), so a year is 56 days. Operators can jump to a date with /season set autumn 3.

The hottest lands (jungles, savannas, deserts and badlands) have no seasons at all: the weather there never turns.

Spring

Fruit trees blossom (cherries and peaches first, then plums, pears and apples) and need bees to set fruit. It's sowing time for most crops, and haskap, the first berry of the year, flowers at winter's end.

Summer

Hot sun dries open fields: dig ditches and use a watering can. Cherries ripen first, then peaches and plums; most wild berries are ripe. On Midsummer Eve, the middle day of summer, the bonfire lights itself at dusk.

Autumn

The leaves turn gold and red. Apples and pears ripen from summer's end, then avocados, lemons, mandarins and olives. Crows are at their boldest over the ripening fields: put up a scarecrow. All Hallows Eve falls four nights before the fair, and the Harvest Fair is the last day of autumn.

Winter

In temperate lands it's cold enough to snow and freeze, and trees frost over while it snows. Most crops wait for spring, but onions, garlic and cabbages are hardy and keep growing. Under glass the seasons can't reach: a greenhouse grows anything, any time. Oranges and lemons ripen through the winter, and the warm-country trees rest until spring.

Tip The almanac charts every fruit tree, berry, crop and fish through the year.
