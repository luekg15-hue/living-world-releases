Genetics & breeding

Animals and crops in Wildhearth carry genes from both parents, so you can breed a herd, flock or crop towards what you want over the generations. There are two kinds of gene, and they work differently.

Blended traits

Most traits are a number, and a youngster gets a blend of its two parents, give or take a little (now and then a lot). Breed your best to your best and each generation drifts your way.

AnimalTraitsIts own gift
Deercoat, antlers, size, temperament-
Cattlesize, growth, fertilityhide: thick-hided cows give extra leather
Pigssize, growth, fertilitylitter: twins up to half the time, and triplets
Chickenssize, growth, fertilitylaying: the best lay half as often again
Sheepsize, growth, fertilityfleece: more wool a clip, and it grows back sooner

Size means more meat (large +1, huge +2). Growth means young ones grow up faster. Fertility means a shorter rest between breedings, and the odd twin. Each trait is passed on by itself, so you can breed for two things at once.

Tip Hold an animal's food and look at it: the line above your hotbar shows its breed, sex and any notable genes, like Merino ram · Wool · Fine fleece · Horned · Heavy fleece.

Genes with two copies

Some genes come in two copies, one from each parent, and work like real ones:

  • Recessive genes only show with two copies. A deer's rare coats (piebald, melanistic and leucistic) and a sheep's black wool are recessive. With one copy the animal looks ordinary but is a hidden carrier.
  • Dominant genes show with just one copy. In cattle and sheep, polled (hornless) is dominant: only an animal with two horned copies grows horns.

Two carriers

Breed two carriers of a recessive gene and, on average, one young in four shows it:

BbBBBwhiteBbcarrierbBbcarrierbbblack

b = a black-wool copy. A white sheep can be BB or Bb; only bb is a black sheep.

Tip Wild herds are families: find one rare deer and its herdmates probably carry the gene too.

Breeds

Every cow, pig, chicken and sheep belongs to a breed, which sets its look and the genes it tends towards, and often gives it a perk (a Texel's extra mutton, a Romanov ewe's triplets, an East Friesian ewe's milk). A youngster takes after one parent's breed. Compare them all in the breed database.

Males and females

Cattle, pigs, chickens and sheep need one of each to breed: a bull and a cow, a boar and a sow, a rooster and a hen, a ram and a ewe. Roosters don't lay, bulls can't be milked, and a ram will charge anyone who hurts his flock.

Crops

Crops have genes too. Let a plant go to seed, save the seed, and cross varieties by planting them side by side. The Seed Saver's Handbook explains pollination, saving seed and breeding, with worked examples.
