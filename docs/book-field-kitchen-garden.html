Kitchen garden

Kitchen garden

Break tall grass and now and then you'll find seeds for the garden: corn, tomatoes, squash and cabbage, and onions and garlic to plant as they are.

Corn KernelsTomato SeedsSquash SeedsCabbage SeedsOnionGarlic

Wild strawberries grow in meadows, plains, forests and cherry groves.

Corn

Grows two blocks tall, so leave it room. Ripe when the tassels turn gold; break either half for 1 to 3 cobs.

→ in a Furnace
→ in a Frying Pan

Roast the cobs, or pop the kernels in a frying pan.

Tomatoes

They stop half-grown until you give them a garden stake to climb.

→2

Pick ripe ones with a right-click; they fruit again.

Squash

Once grown, a squash plant sends vines snaking out over open grass, up to ten blocks.

Vines flower, and with bees about every flower sets a squash. Right-click ripe ones.

→ in a Furnace

The first frost kills the vines, dropping any ripe squash.

Winter crops

Onions, garlic and cabbages are hardy: they keep growing through winter, when other crops wait for spring.

OnionGarlicCabbage

Plant an onion or a garlic clove as it is.

Turnips, sweet potatoes & chillies

Turnips like the cold and grow on slowly through winter; sweet potatoes and chillies want warmth and rest in winter.

TurnipSweet PotatoChilli

Seeds come from their produce, slips from a sweet potato, and now and then from grass.

Low, leafy plants for grass or farmland. They flower in late spring and fruit into summer: right-click to pick.

Hot soups

Soups and stews come from the cooking pot. They fill you up and give you Warmth.

Tomato SoupSquash SoupCorn ChowderHarvest Stew

While Warmth lasts the winter cold can't get at you, and you thaw out as if by a fire. A stew lasts longest.

Saving seed

Craft a corn cob into 3 kernels, a tomato into 2 seeds and a squash into 4 seeds to sow again.

Going to seed

Left standing when ripe, a plant goes to seed. Harvest it then for seed with its genes (and fewer crops).

An ordinary harvest gives back the seed you sowed.

→

Hold seed and look at a plant to read its genes. A tin compares packets.

Save from your best. Corn, squash and cabbage cross with neighbours; bees carry it further. Settled lines earn names.

Wild blueberries

Low bushes carpeting northern woods, heaths and meadows. Pick the dusty blue berries all summer; they ripen again.

Wild Blueberry BushWild Blueberries

White bells in spring, blazing red in autumn. Bushes spread slowly.

Haskap

A hardy shrub of the cold north, two blocks tall. Its long blue berries are the first fruit of the year.

Haskap SaplingHaskap Berries

Pale yellow flowers at winter's end, ripe in late spring, gold in autumn.

Cloudberries

Ankle-high in bogs, swamps and snowy tundra. Each stem holds one berry: hard and red at first, soft and gold when ripe.

Cloudberry PlantCloudberriesCloudberry Jam

White flowers in late spring, ripe in high summer. Spreads into bog patches.

Lingonberries

A low evergreen under the pines. Tart red berries ripen in autumn and hang on under the snow.

Lingonberry BushLingonberriesLingonberry Jam

Pink bells in spring; leaves bronze in winter but never fall.

Raspberries

Canes two blocks tall at the edges of broadleaf woods. Soft red berries in high summer; new canes come up around them.

Raspberry CanesRaspberriesRaspberry Jam

Yellow leaves in autumn, bare brown canes in winter.

Blackberries

A thorny bramble that creeps outwards. It snags and scratches whoever pushes through. Black berries from late summer.

Blackberry BrambleBlackberriesBlackberry Jam

Ripens unevenly: red and black berries together. Foxes slip through unharmed.

Elder

A tall shrub of woods and hedges. In early summer, pick its cream flower plates for elderflowers, or leave them for berries.

ElderElderflowersElderberries

You can't have both: a picked elder has no berries that year. Raw elderberries upset the stomach.

Sea buckthorn

A thorny, silver-leaved shrub of beaches and stony shores. Its branches are packed with orange berries from autumn into winter.

Sea BuckthornSea Buckthorn BerriesSea Buckthorn Juice

Grows in sand and gravel. Press the berries for juice.

Wild mushrooms

In forest and taiga: reishi, lion's mane and turkey tail on trunks, chaga on birch, shiitake on fallen logs, maitake at tree roots.

ReishiLion’s ManeTurkey TailChagaShiitakeMaitake

Break one to pick it; replant it where it grows. Maitake also comes up under trees in autumn.

Tinctures

Two of one mushroom, a glass bottle and a honey bottle make a tincture.

→

Raised beds

Plank frames full of rich soil. Crops grow as on farmland and nothing tramples them. Beds side by side join up.

→2

Water one end and it spreads. Summer dries beds fast; winter never.

Ollas and mulch

Bury a clay olla in a bed and fill it with a bucket: it keeps the soil round it moist for days.

→

Wheat or hay on a bed is mulch: it dries half as fast.

The wormery

Dig with a spade and now and then a worm turns up, more after rain. Put some in a wormery.

→

Feed it greens (scraps) and browns (paper, leaves, straw) evenly, or it sours or dries.

Castings and tea

EarthwormWorm CastingsWorm Tea

Castings: +5 fertility, +2 round about. The best soil food there is.

Worm tea from the tap: pour it on a bed to feed the soil and lift the crops.

Happy worms multiply. Bring the bin in for a hard winter. A worm on you is bait.
