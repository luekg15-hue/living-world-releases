The Seed Saver's Handbook

Breeding

Selection

Each year, save seed only from the best few plants.

yield, by generation: 8 → 9 → 10 → 11 → 12

Slow and steady: about a step a year.

Breeding true

Two different copies and the seedlings scatter. Two matched copies pass on every time.

Size: genes 11 and 11 → shows as 11.0

Save a line from itself a few years and it settles, and earns a name.

Crossing lines

Cross two settled lines and every seedling gets one copy from each: an even first generation.

line A (14, 14) × line B (6, 6)

A first cross grows strongly: hybrid vigour.

The second year

Save seed from that cross and the copies shuffle again: some take after one grandparent, some the other.

the cross (14, 6) × the cross (14, 6)

Here are the surprises. Pick the best and start a new line.

Vigour and inbreeding

Plants with mixed copies grow stronger. Crops that cross freely gain most from it.

Inbred too long, those same crops grow weak.

Selfers like wheat hardly mind inbreeding.

Mutations

About one copy in 25 shifts a step as seed is made; now and then, two or three.

Once in about 120 savings a sport appears: one trait leaps, and the line becomes a named heirloom.

The fair and the pedlar give heirlooms too.
